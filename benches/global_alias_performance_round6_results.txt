Implementation
--------------
Both branches now separate the small pair-add dispatch from the large sum-merge
and general normalization paths. Distinct normalized terms are ordered once
and written with one final-size reservation and one canonical packed header.
The final constructor reuses an existing Add buffer directly or transfers the
old output's raw buffer. It does not initialize an empty sum first. Assembly
inspection confirmed that the earlier path reserved/wrote an empty header,
then cleared it and reserved again for the pair. Equal terms, dirty operands,
coefficients, and alias-bearing operands retain their appropriate normalizers.
Canonical minimal header widths remain an invariant.

Also shared: Add capacity growth now passes size-len to Vec::reserve (whose
argument is additional capacity relative to length, not capacity). Owned Ord
checks byte equality before constructing views; unequal global keys retain
semantic alias comparison and metadata tie-breaking. AtomView::to_owned
constructs a typed owned atom directly, avoiding a temporary Zero plus the
general setter and its drop handling.

Global AliasHandle stores its validated generation-aware packed token once.
Ownership collection returns the decoded child boundary instead of parsing
it again to skip the child, and recognizes consecutive repeated owners.
Direct transparent-alias equality borrows the single retained owner's body
before constructing full views. The chosen owner container stores one Arc
inline in its sidecar and allocates a sorted Vec only for multiple distinct
owners; it collects replacements before releasing old references and reuses
an existing sidecar. Copying a child retains only owners actually referenced
by that child. RawAtom's own Eq/Hash remain byte-oriented.

Measurement method
------------------
Final runs use six rounds, three samples per workload, 100 ms target, CPU 6
(SMT sibling 18), adjacent variants with balanced rotated ordering. The storage
ablation uses four balanced rounds and 70 ms targets; the product ablation uses
six rounds and 80 ms targets. No own builds, tests, or
profiles ran during timings. compare_alias_workloads.py --quiet gates each
complete group on both CPUs below 15% busy over one second and no observed rustc.
It retries the complete group if any run has process CPU/wall below 95%, sibling
activity above 20%, or rustc before/after execution. Acceptance never depends on
operation times. Rejected raw observations remain available.

Times are medians of within-round medians; percentage changes are medians of
within-round ratios and need not equal ratios of displayed times. Observed
round ranges are not confidence intervals. Boost/frequency scaling and other
machine activity remain uncontrolled; the checks reduce observable interference
but cannot detect every short-lived background process.

Pair addition measures x*y +2*x^2*y^2, including result allocation/destruction.
Owned comparisons use separate buffers; view comparisons include construction
of the views. Hash timings include creating/finishing seeded AHash. Registration
is outside alias timing. These are operations, not map lookup benchmarks.
Expansion uses the same four workloads on main, global plain inputs, and global
aliased inputs; correctness checks compare actual expanded results.

Product-normalization investigation
-----------------------------------
The sampled baseline ran normalize_product in approximately 1.44-1.48 us on
main and 1.87-1.94 us on global. No observed rustc or lost samples occurred;
SMT sibling activity was at most 3.3%. These are profiling runs, not final
quiet timing results.

Main AtomView is 24 bytes and compound views 16; global is 32 and 24 respectively.
The ordinary owned Atom is 32 bytes on main and 40 on global. Main passes a
power view's data/length in registers; global materializes its extra owner
context and passes the24-byte view indirectly. get_base_exp returns 48 bytes
on main and 64 on global. The global caller reloads wider aggregates across
separate scalar return stores. Samples concentrate at those return/reload
sites in merge_factors. This is consistent with register pressure and possibly
store-forwarding costs; sampling does not prove a particular hardware stall.
The inspected NumView getter and packed coefficient decoder have unchanged
instruction sequences.

Both branches originally used #[inline], but their measured optimized binaries
still contained out-of-line get_base and get_base_exp calls. Forcing these two
getters inline removes those calls and permits caller-side scalarization. The
tradeoff is code size: merge_factors grows 47.8% on main and 28.1% on global;
whole normalize grows 3.5% and 4.6%. It does not reduce the expression's byte size.
Product normalization also moved a completed owned factor into SmallVec after
filling a local buffer. Filling the final slot directly avoids that completed
factor relocation. A recycled-buffer placement and expression-byte memcpy
remain, so this does not claim to eliminate all copying.

Alias storage experiments
-------------------------
The selected storage change keeps byte vectors inline and stores one alias owner directly in the optional ownership sidecar. This is the `legacy` variant below, committed as 6bcf14b7. The compact Atom experiment was rejected after it slowed the tested ordinary paths despite reducing Atom's size. These measurements compare storage variants against the round-5 global-alias core at 5516b46d; they are not a comparison against main or measurements of subsequent pair-constructor changes.

All sizes below are bytes on the tested 64-bit target. Main has Atom=32 and AtomView=24. Every global-alias variant retains AtomView=32: its ownership context is an 8-byte reference needed to borrow alias bodies safely.

| Variant | Storage and owners | RawAtom | Atom | Alias sidecar | Allocations for singleton ownership |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| core | Inline Vec + Option<Box<Vec<Arc>>> | 32 | 40 | 24 | 2 |
| compact | Plain(Vec) / Aliased(Box<{Vec, Vec<Arc>}>) | 24 | 32 | 48 | 2 |
| inline | Compact storage + Single(Arc) / Many(Vec<Arc>) owners | 24 | 32 | 48 | 1 |
| legacy (selected) | Inline Vec + Option<Box<Single/Many owners>> | 32 | 40 | 24 | 1 |

Sidecar sizes exclude the expression-byte allocation, owner-vector element allocation, alias handle/body, and allocator overhead. Alias-free atoms allocate no ownership sidecar. The selected design starts collection with an inline Single owner, including when copying a one-owner child from a multi-owner parent, and allocates an owner vector only on the second distinct token. Borrowed contexts remain thin references; token ordering, generation identity, and selective retention of only referenced owners are preserved. Existing owner boxes can be reused when replacing an ownership context.

The trial used four paired rounds, three samples per run, a 70 ms target, CPU 6, and rotated variant order. All 768 accepted samples (64 complete groups, 256 runs) were audited against raw output. Eight complete groups were rejected using telemetry alone. Accepted process CPU/wall was 99.45–99.67%; frequency scaling and boost remained enabled. Times are medians of round medians. Changes are medians of within-round ratios, so they need not equal ratios of the displayed times. Negative changes mean faster. Group figures are geometric means of the workloads' paired medians, not estimates of application-wide speedup.

The compact variants consistently lost on ordinary arithmetic: compact/core +8.25% and inline/core +7.16%. The smaller Atom also did not rescue small keys: owned equal Eq was +70.90% and +78.01%, respectively. Inline owners helped the compact alias operations versus compact alone (group -5.13%; multi-owner child copying -10.86%), but did not overcome its ordinary-path penalty.

The selected legacy layout stayed close to core on the ordinary arithmetic group (+0.54%) and improved alias operations by -3.50%. Its alias-power multiplication gain was consistent across rounds: -6.80%, observed range [-7.22%, -6.63%]. Multi-owner child copying improved by a median -3.84%, but its range [-7.23%, +0.23%] crossed zero. Pair addition was effectively unchanged in this trial: -0.27%, range [-3.88%, +0.64%]. The selected layout did not win every case: small owned equal Ord regressed +8.09%, range [+7.34%, +8.32%]. Its small Hash median was -6.88%, but the range [-7.48%, +35.73%] was unstable. These ranges describe four observed rounds; they are not confidence intervals.

The experiment shows that removing eight bytes from Atom was insufficient to improve these workloads. Compact storage also introduced a storage-kind decision for byte access and pointer indirection for aliased bytes, and changed layout and generated code more broadly. Those are plausible explanations for its penalties; this trial does not identify branch misses, cache misses, or any one mechanism as the cause. Likewise, singleton owner-vector allocation is structurally eliminated in the selected design, but its timing changes also include ownership-container and box-reuse code changes. We should not attribute every measured difference solely to one removed allocation.

The selected layout is a measured compromise, not a claim of optimality. Atom remains 40 bytes and AtomView 32 bytes, compared with main's 32 and 24. Multi-owner atoms still allocate a vector and do selective token lookup; all alias-bearing owned atoms still need a sidecar. Possible future experiments include a shared immutable owner set with a compact subset representation for copied children (must avoid retaining unrelated owners), a custom tagged allocation/header layout that keeps byte access direct (requires a substantially more invasive representation and safety review), or a view API that separates ordinary views from ownership-bearing views (must preserve borrowed-body lifetimes and avoid making common callers pay conversion costs). None of these alternatives has been benchmarked here; the failed compact experiment argues for measuring byte-access cost as well as object size before adopting them.

Interpretation and remaining work
---------------------------------
The measured no-alias arithmetic geometric-mean gap is +14.05% against updated
main. Product normalization is +11.92% (main 1428.359 ns, global 1593.268 ns),
compared with +28.93% in the preceding isolated baseline experiment. This is a
comparison across the two recorded runs, each with its own updated-main
reference. Differentiation remains +26.01% against main (5586.790 vs 7001.435 ns):
shared improvements speed up main as well. Its remaining gap is not closed by
removing power-getter return copies alone.

Small pair addition is now 52.820 ns versus main's 47.099 ns (+11.49% paired),
compared with 83.838 ns on the saved old-alias branch. Ordinary owned equality
is near parity; small view equality is +14.71% (3.394 vs 2.966 ns), small owned
ordering +8.23% (2.909 vs 2.689 ns), and small owned hashing +26.65% (2.917 vs
2.309 ns). These tiny operations retain appreciable relative overhead even
where the absolute difference is below one nanosecond. Large owned equality
is near parity and large hashing is +1.73% in this workload.

Alias copying gains against the saved old-alias branch include repeated
multi-owner copying -57.36%, copying a child from a multi-owner expression
-28.94%, and small alias-power copying -26.70%. Alias/plain comparison -16.77%
and alias-power multiplication -16.07% also improve. These cumulative deltas
include the main updates and all optimizations since the old source.

Matched native expansion produces the same encoded output sizes in all three
variants and validates equivalent expanded results. Actual aliases add median
paired overheads +2.26%, +1.94%, -0.07%, +1.62% over the same global branch with
plain inputs. The no-alias branch-versus-main expansion gaps remain 14-18%.
The near-zero large-power difference is within its observed range and is not
evidence of a reliable alias speedup.

Selected-source profiles confirm that out-of-line power-getter calls are gone.
The remaining derivative profile spans normalization, temporary-buffer drops,
buffer construction/extension, and derivative traversal. A separate static
candidate forces the Indeterminate equality wrapper inline: main already
inlines this comparison at recursive derivative entries while global emits a
call. That helper accounts for only 0.71% of the two global derivative profiles,
so it cannot by itself explain the remaining 26% gap. Argument-derivative
entries are 40 bytes on main and 48 on global and still undergo copies. The
identified tuple-push block accounts for roughly 0.12-0.34% of whole-workload
samples; this is not the bulk of the remaining gap and does not count every
view or owned-buffer copy elsewhere. This candidate
and a guard to skip the completed-group alias scan for singleton products are
kept in the cache for later controlled experiments, not applied to these results.

Final sampled profiles observed no rustc before/after any run. Product profiles
had at most 5.77% sibling activity. One main derivative recording had 14.02%
sibling activity and an elevated first sample; the other main recording is the
cleaner comparison. Profiling is diagnostic and does not replace the final
paired timing tables. No specific hardware stall is claimed to be proven.
