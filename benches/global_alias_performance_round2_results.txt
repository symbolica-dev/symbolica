Outcome
-------
The pairwise addition regression is fixed: 61.3% less time than the previous
optimized code, and 50.0% less than main. Construction is 6.0% faster than main.
The nine no-alias workloads use 18.1% less time than the previous optimization
and 28.0% less than the merged global-alias baseline (geometric means).

In this same run, aggregate overhead versus main falls from 24.6% to 2.1%.
That aggregate is strongly influenced by pair addition. Excluding pair addition,
overhead falls from 24.2% to 11.6%; several workloads remain 10–21% slower than
main. These unweighted workload aggregates do not estimate application speedup.

The small alias-power copy regression versus the previous optimization remains.
Diagnostic counters show essentially unchanged instructions (-0.25%) and
branches, with about 3% more cycles. The hot routines are unchanged or smaller.
This suggests throughput/code-layout or allocator effects rather than new
algorithmic work; the specific cause is not established. All four live-alias
workloads remain faster than the original merged global baseline.

Changes and profiling evidence
------------------------------
- Borrow ordinary views in structural/factor comparisons and semantic equality.
  Resolve transparent aliases only when an actual Alias view is encountered.
- Skip recycling cleanup when a buffer owns no alias handles; retain the cold
  ownership-release path even when normalization already cleared subtree flags.
- Add distinct normalized nonnumeric terms directly in canonical order, before
  allocating sum-merge scratch atoms. Numeric, matching, dirty, and alias-bearing
  inputs keep the existing normalization rules.
- Read alias presence from the encoded header; avoid redundant ownership resets
  after clearing and copying ordinary compound expressions.
- Decode only a power's base when requested; initialize empty Add/Mul headers
  with one four-byte copy.

Final perf diagnostics versus main show about 46% fewer retired instructions
for pair addition. In product normalization, cmp_factors falls from 11.5% of
round-1 samples to 2.0%; cmp falls from 4.3% to 1.5%. Assembly confirms that
the unconditional comparator-entry copies and duplicate ownership cleanup
after copying an ordinary subtree are gone.

Remaining normalization/expansion instruction overhead is about 11% versus main.
Samples concentrate on moving temporary owned atoms, including the factor
SmallVec and power-construction buffers. Atom is 40 bytes versus main's 32.
This identifies further work in normalization's scratch ownership/copies; it
does not establish that the remaining gap is unavoidable. Alias-store lookups
are not the measured no-alias bottleneck.

A separate eight-round, two-build comparison confirmed the last batch improves
all nine workloads versus intermediate commit 6ba7ea56: 4.9% geometric mean
less time, with individual improvements of 2.7–11.5%.

Method and retained evidence
----------------------------
Twelve paired rounds x three approximately 100 ms samples per workload/variant;
36 samples each. Four no-alias variants and three live-alias variants rotate
order, balanced across all positions. Binaries run sequentially on logical CPU 6
of an AMD Ryzen 9 5900X. No concurrent builds/tests/profiles during timing;
normal desktop background activity remains. Setup/calibration is untimed,
and result destruction is timed. Rust 1.99.0, release LTO, one codegen unit,
native CPU flags, identical dependency versions and default features.

perf uses cycles:u at 1999 Hz (4 x 300ms) and hardware counters (5 x 500ms),
also sequentially on CPU 6. Counters include setup/calibration, so normalized
per-operation counts are diagnostic upper estimates. Leaf-IP sampling can skid;
it locates candidate hot code rather than proving a precise causal attribution.
